# Reverse NIH in GCC/KSA

## One-Line Thesis

Reverse NIH in wealthy emerging economies is a **legitimacy deficit**, not a frugality discount — and breaking that cycle is the untold story of Vision 2030-era KSA.

## Three-Act Structure

**Act 1 — Reverse NIH as default.** GCC/KSA's historical reflex: Western = best. McKinsey, BCG, Big Tech. Hire the brand, not the capability.

**Act 2 — The scapegoat dynamic.** CEOs hire McKinsey not because they're good, but because they're *insulated*. If it fails, "we hired the best in the world." The consultancy is an insurance policy, not a solution provider.

**Act 3 — The awakening.** Vision 2030 pushes local content. The shift from "hire Western" to "build local." But Reverse NIH doesn't disappear overnight — the legitimacy discount persists even as procurement behavior changes.

## Novel Contribution

Extends **Lichtenthaler & Ernst (2006)** into a new context. Challenges **Govindarajan & Ramamurti (2011)** — the direction of bias is NOT always tied to economic asymmetry. In wealthy GCC economies, it's a *legitimacy* problem, not a cost one. This angle has not been written.

## Methodology

**SLR + Conceptual Framework with Illustrative Vignettes**

- PRISMA-style SLR to formally prove the gap (Durugbo et al. found 110 GCC innovation papers, none touch this framing)
- No organizational access needed
- Illustrative vignettes: SDAIA procurement, McKinsey engagements, Vision 2030 local content targets
- Research propositions for future empirical testing
- Qualitative interviews as follow-up (once thesis has attention, decision-makers come to you)

### Updated Contribution Positioning

The phenomenon is well-documented in BOTH HR and procurement. What's missing is NOT documentation — it's **theoretical integration**. The paper's contribution is:
1. **Unifying two siloed literatures** — HR/talent and procurement/consulting don't cite each other but describe the same phenomenon
2. **Naming and defining the construct** — "Reverse NIH" / "liability of localness" / "foreign presumption advantage" with a nomological network
3. **Formal falsifiable propositions** — The literature is descriptive; no one has said "here are testable propositions about when this activates, intensifies, or breaks"

### KEY CORRECTION: Conceptual novelty recalibrated

"Reverse NIH" / "buy-in syndrome" is NOT a new construct — it exists in KM literature. What IS novel:

1. **Domain extension** — Buy-in syndrome has never been applied beyond KM tasks (never to procurement or hiring)
2. **Context extension** — Has only been studied in Western R&D, never in GCC institutional economics
3. **Synthesis** — No paper has unified GCC HR literature + GCC procurement literature under buy-in/NIH theory
4. **The PFE distinction** — Proudly Found Elsewhere is the *healthy* open innovation practice. Our Reverse NIH is the *pathological irrational* foreign preference. Most of the 165 PFE papers conflate these — we separate them.
