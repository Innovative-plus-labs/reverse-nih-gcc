Systematic Literature Review — Search Strategy & Pipeline

Search Architecture

Six parallel sources spanning four literature clusters:

Database searches

  1. ScienceDirect (title/abstract/keywords): "not invented here syndrome" and "Reverse not invented here syndrome" — 38 records
  2. Semantic Scholar API (12 targeted queries): NIH/buy-in and innovation attitudes, GCC expatriate preference, GCC consulting dependency, Saudi local content (LCGPA, IKTVA, Vision 2030), Latin America xenocentrism — 48 records
  3. Google Scholar (manual screen): NIH and procurement/consulting/hiring intersections, Reverse NIH and buy-in in emerging economies, GCC local content — 28 records
  4. Crossref / OpenAlex (API): LCGPA, Nazaha, local content policy, Latin America innovation — 12 records
  5. Scopus (prior session): GCC HR institutional analysis, GCC procurement — 21 records

Supplementary sources

  1. Manual snowballing from reference lists of core papers (Lichtenthaler & Ernst 2006; Sidani & Al Ariss, 2014; Jones, 2019) — 16 records

Total records identified: 163 from six sources across four clusters.

Inclusion Criteria

  • Peer-reviewed journal articles, academic books, book chapters, and doctoral dissertations
  • Cluster A: studies addressing attitudes toward external knowledge, technology, or expertise at organizational level (NIH, buy-in, PFE)
  • Clusters B and C: studies addressing GCC-context human capital, talent management, nationalization, local content, or professional services procurement
  • Cluster D: studies addressing foreign preference, xenocentrism, or liability of localness in emerging economies outside GCC
  • English-language publications, 1982–2025

Exclusion Criteria

  • Medical/biomedical false positives ("NIH" as National Institutes of Health)
  • Studies addressing "reverse innovation" (innovation flows low→high income)
  • Individual-level NIH without organizational framing
  • Opinion pieces, blog posts, trade magazines
  • Non-English publications

Screening and Selection

163 records → after deduplication: 149 unique records. Title/abstract screening eliminated 81 (38 medical false positives, 18 wrong concept, 6 non-English, 12 non-peer-reviewed, 7 unrelated). 68 sought for full-text — 15 unavailable (paywall). Full-text review of 53 eliminated 12 (5 no NIH construct, 4 tangential, 3 low quality).

Final corpus: 41 records. 18 Cluster A (NIH/buy-in), 8 Cluster B (GCC HR), 5 Cluster C (GCC procurement), 6 Cluster D (emerging parallels), 4 methodology/mechanism.

Cross-Citation Finding

All 6 inter-cluster cells = 0. A→B, A→C, B→A, B→C, C→A, C→B all zero. Clusters B→C (GCC HR → GCC procurement) was the most surprising: two literatures studying the same phenomenon in the same region, not citing each other. See full cross-citation matrix.

Synthesis Methodology

Given zero cross-citation, conventional meta-analysis was impossible. Used construct-level thematic synthesis (Thomas & Harden, 2008): extracted phenomenon descriptions, mechanisms, and moderators from each paper; translated into shared theoretical vocabulary from NIH/buy-in framework; identified where same mechanism appeared under different names.

Synthesis identified three mechanisms present in both HR and procurement literatures: (1) default distrust of local / default trust of foreign, (2) decision-maker self-protection, (3) capability suppression through exclusion.
