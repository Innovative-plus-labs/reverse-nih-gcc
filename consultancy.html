Reverse NIH — Content Track & Consultancy Offering

1. Content Track: From Paper to Public Conversation

Target Audiences

  • Primary: GCC government procurement officers, HR directors, Vision 2030 program managers, LCGPA officials
  • Secondary: GCC business owners, local consultancy founders, Saudi/UAE entrepreneurship ecosystem
  • Tertiary: Western consultancies operating in GCC, international development organizations

    • Content Pillars (4-week launch sequence)

    Week 1: "Name the Problem"

    **Core message:** There is a name for what you've been experiencing. It's not "international best practice." It's not "the global talent pool." It's Reverse NIH — and naming it is the first step to fixing it.

  • LinkedIn post 1: "Why does your organization hire McKinsey to design strategies your own team could design? There's a name for this. 🧵" (thread: 5 tweets, introduce the construct)
  • Carousel: "6 Signs Your Organization Has Reverse NIH" — visual, shareable, diagnostic
  • Arabic version: Mirror content for Arabic-speaking GCC decision-makers

    • Week 2: "Show the Evidence"

    **Core message:** This isn't opinion. 41 peer-reviewed papers across 4 research clusters document the same phenomenon. The data is in.

  • LinkedIn post: "We reviewed 41 academic papers. Zero of them cite each other. Here's what that means for you." — data visualization of cross-citation gap as metaphor for organizational silos
  • Whiteboard infographic: PRISMA flow diagram → accessible format showing "how we found what nobody was connecting"
  • Twitter/X thread: "41 papers. 4 silos. 0 cross-citations. The Reverse NIH research gap, visualized."

    • Week 3: "Explain the Mechanisms"

    **Core message:** Three mechanisms keep Reverse NIH running. You can break each one.

  • LinkedIn post: "The 3 reasons foreign always beats local in GCC procurement — and what to do about each" (default distrust, procurement-as-insurance, self-reinforcing exclusion)
  • Video script: 3-minute explainer — each mechanism with a concrete GCC example (Saudi McKinsey spending, education reform cycle, ghost employment)
  • Carousel: "The Self-Reinforcing Exclusion Trap" — visual explainer of the track record paradox

    • Week 4: "Solutions & Propositions"

    **Core message:** Here's what we can test. Here's what LCGPA, Vision 2030, and organizational leaders can do.

  • LinkedIn post: "Vision 2030's local content mandate is working. But it's producing a trap. Here's the data."
  • Consultation landing page: "Reverse NIH Audit for GCC Organizations" — offer a diagnostic workshop
  • Newsletter: Deep dive into 6 falsifiable propositions — what they mean for practitioners

    2. Consultancy Offering: Reverse NIH Diagnostic & Intervention

    Service Architecture

    Tier 1: Reverse NIH Diagnostic (2-Week Sprint)

    **Price:** $15,000-$25,000

    **For:** Single GCC organization (government entity, SOE, large private corp)

    Deliverables:

    1. **Procurement Audit:** Analysis of 3 years of procurement data — contract awards by provider origin, category, value. Identify knowledge-intensity gradient pattern and mandate compliance vs. symbolic compliance

    2. **HR Hiring Audit:** Analysis of 3 years of hiring data — senior/strategic hires by credential origin, salary premiums by university origin, promotion velocity by nationality

    3. **Gatekeeper Survey:** Anonymous survey of 20-50 procurement officers and hiring managers — vignette-based experiment measuring default trust/distrust asymmetry (P1 operationalization)

    4. **Reverse NIH Scorecard:** Composite score across mechanisms — visual dashboard comparing organization to sector benchmarks (where available)

    5. **Executive Briefing:** 90-minute presentation of findings with mechanism-specific recommendations

    **Toolkit:** Adapted conjoint experiment from P1 operationalization, knowledge-intensity taxonomy coding guide, procurement origin classifier

    Tier 2: Intervention Design (6-Week Engagement)

    **Price:** $50,000-$80,000

    **For:** Organization that has completed Diagnostic and wants structural intervention

    Deliverables:

    1. **Mechanism-Matched Intervention Design:**

  • Default distrust → Blind screening protocols, structured evaluation frameworks
  • Procurement-as-insurance → Outcome-based accountability redesign (from process compliance to delivery metrics)
  • Self-reinforcing exclusion → Staged contracting program, local provider track record registry

    • 2. **Pilot Design:** 6-month pilot of 2-3 interventions with control group comparison

    3. **Measurement Framework:** KPIs mapped to our 6 propositions — pre/post procurement origin ratios, gatekeeper survey pre/post, contract layer analysis (prime vs. sub vs. advisory)

    4. **Policy Alignment:** Intervention design mapped to LCGPA compliance requirements, Vision 2030 targets, Nitaqat bands

    **Toolkit:** Intervention protocol manual, measurement dashboard template, LCGPA compliance mapping matrix

    Tier 3: Sector-Wide Transformation (3-6 Month Retainer)

    **Price:** $150,000-$300,000

    **For:** Government entities (LCGPA, Ministry of Investment, Vision 2030 program offices)

    Deliverables:

    1. **Multi-Organization Baseline:** Diagnostic across 10-50 procuring entities — sector-wide Reverse NIH mapping

    2. **Policy Instrument Evaluation:** Effectiveness analysis of LCGPA 10% price preference, Mandatory List, IKTVA local content scoring — measuring mandate moderation (P6 substitution effect)

    3. **Longitudinal Design:** 3-year data collection protocol for natural experiment (Vision 2030 as treatment, pre/post mandate comparison)

    4. **Institutional Redesign:** Specific recommendations for Nazaha procurement integrity integration, Etimad platform modifications, accountability structure reform

    5. **Academic Partnership:** Joint publication of findings — transforms consulting deliverable into peer-reviewed empirical test of propositions

    Competitive Position

  • vs. McKinsey/BCG: They sell the foreign expertise our framework diagnoses as problematic. Our offering is diagnostic, not prescriptive — we help you know *when* to hire McKinsey and *when* to trust local
  • vs. LCGPA consultants: They focus on compliance (are you hitting 45%?). We focus on mechanism (is your 45% real or symbolic?)
  • vs. HR consultancies: They do talent management. We do institutional diagnosis — the organizational-level condition that shapes TM outcomes

    • Revenue Model

  • Tier 1: Project-based ($15-25k per diagnostic)
  • Tier 2: Engagement-based ($50-80k per intervention design)
  • Tier 3: Retainer-based ($15-30k/month)
  • IP asset: The Reverse NIH framework, diagnostic toolkit, and proposition-testing methodology are the defensible asset — not the consulting hours
  • Scalability: Train local GCC consultants (Saudi, Emirati, Bahraini) in the framework → franchise diagnostic methodology → scale beyond founder's time

    • Launch Strategy

    1. **Month 1-2:** Publish paper → content track launch → build credibility as "the Reverse NIH researcher"

    2. **Month 3-4:** Offer 2-3 free/reduced diagnostics to pilot organizations (Saudi ministry, UAE SOE, Bahraini fund) → refine toolkit → collect case studies

    3. **Month 5-6:** Launch Tier 1 commercially → public case studies (anonymized) → content track enters solutions phase

    4. **Month 7-12:** First Tier 2 engagement → academic partnership for joint publication → begin Tier 3 conversations with LCGPA/Nazaha

    Key Risks

  • Framework adoption risk: "Reverse NIH" may not gain traction as a term. Mitigation: anchor in Lichtenthaler & Ernst (2006) and NIH (1982), both established constructs
  • Political sensitivity: Diagnosing GCC procurement as systematically biased may be perceived as criticism. Mitigation: frame as capability-building opportunity ("you've invested in capability, now remove the institutional friction blocking it from delivering")
  • Western consultancy retaliation: McKinsey/BCG may perceive the framework as threat. Mitigation: position as complementary — "we help you know *when* to hire them, not *whether* to hire them"
